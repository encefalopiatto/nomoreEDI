Responses you are writing

Why can't I edit some fields?

The original message dictates how it must be answered: some fields must be copied over exactly (those are locked and grey), and some are yours to decide (those are open). The node checks your answer against those instructions before it signs and sends anything.

Who we are

What is a key fingerprint?

Your company owns a key pair: a private key that stays on this machine and signs every outgoing message, and a public key that anyone can use to check those signatures. The fingerprint is the public key's short name — partners can read it back to you over the phone to make sure they have the right one.

Partners and their addresses

Why does this list matter so much?

Messages only ever travel to addresses on this list. A message merely claiming a new address never changes this list by itself — that claim becomes a proposal under Decisions, and the rule is to confirm it through a different channel (phone your contact) before approving. This single habit is what defeats the classic "our bank account changed" fraud.

Rulebooks in force

What is a rulebook?

A rulebook is the agreed requirements for one kind of document from one partner — written twice on purpose: once as a plain sentence for people, once in a small checking language for the machine. Both are shown below, so you can verify they say the same thing. A rulebook is known by its fingerprint: a code computed from its exact content, so two rulebooks with the same fingerprint are guaranteed identical.

House rules for approving changes

Can these be loosened?

Only up to a floor that is built into the program itself: changes to addresses, keys, or anything touching money or company identifiers can never be applied without a human, even if someone edits this settings file. The list of "sensitive words" below is what triggers the extra caution.

Quarantine

What ends up here?

Files that failed the identity check — they claim to come from someone, but are not signed with that company's key. They are locked away unopened and kept as evidence. Nothing inside them was trusted, shown for approval, or acted on.

The diary (audit log)

How can I trust this diary?

Every line records one event — a proposal noticed, a decision made, a change applied, a file refused — and carries a seal computed from its own content plus the seal of the line before it. Editing any past line breaks every seal after it. The button re-checks the whole chain.
